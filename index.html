From the street to your screen

Four pieces work together every time you open a web page.

The service line

A cable, fibre or mobile signal brings the internet from your provider into the building.

The modem

Translates that incoming signal into a form your home equipment can use.

The router

Shares the connection between all your devices and directs each request to the right place.

Your devices

Phones, laptops and televisions connect by Wi-Fi or by a cable and ask for pages and videos.

Why Wi-Fi feels strong in one room and weak in another

Wi-Fi travels as radio waves, and those waves weaken with distance and with obstacles. Thick walls, floors, mirrors and large appliances all absorb or reflect the signal, so the same router can feel excellent in one room and poor in the next.

Many people place the router in a corner, behind furniture or in a cupboard because it looks tidy. A central, open and slightly raised position usually spreads the signal more evenly through a home.

Neighbouring networks can also add interference, especially in apartment buildings. Understanding these basics helps you try sensible fixes before assuming something is broken.

A calm order for fixing a slow connection

Work through these steps from simple to more involved, and stop when the problem is solved.

  1. Check whether it is one device or all

    If only one device is slow, the problem is probably with that device, not the network.

  2. Restart the router

    Turn it off for about thirty seconds and switch it back on. This clears many temporary glitches.

  3. Move closer or move the router

    Test near the router. If speed improves, the signal may simply be struggling to reach your room.

  4. Try a cable

    A wired connection shows whether Wi-Fi is the weak link for fixed devices like a desktop or television.

  5. Update and check settings

    Keep the router's software updated according to the maker's instructions, and contact your provider if the issue continues.

Three habits for a steadier home network

Secure the Wi-Fi

Use a strong, unique password and change default ones, so that strangers cannot join your network.

Place it well

Position the router centrally, off the floor and away from thick walls and other electronics.

Reduce the load

Large downloads and many streams at once share one connection. Spreading them out keeps everything smoother.

Connected homes

Networks quietly support work, learning and entertainment.

Common questions

Does Techvanta sell internet plans or equipment?

No. Techvanta is an informational website. We do not sell or recommend internet plans, routers or any other products or services, and we do not provide technical support.

Is a modem the same as a router?

No. A modem connects your home to your provider, while a router shares that connection with your devices. Some providers supply a single box that does both jobs.

Why is my internet slower in the evening?

Many households use the internet at the same time in the evening, which can increase congestion. Your own home may also have more devices active then.

Should I leave my router on all the time?

Most people do. An occasional restart can help with glitches, but there is usually no need to switch it off every night.
